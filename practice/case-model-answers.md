## Case 1 · Pedro & María's scooter start-up (Units 2–3)

**Facts.** Pedro and María want to launch an e-scooter rental platform (GPS app, mobile payment, park on public roads). Strategy: (a) launch, (b) expand, (c) maximise value, (d) <b>sell to a third party</b>. They need <b>Business Angels</b> first and later <b>venture capital</b>. María has an important personal wealth and wants to <b>limit her liability</b>.


### (1) What type of company could they create, given their strategy?

A <b>capital company</b> (S.L. or S.A.). Their plan needs (i) <b>limited liability</b>, (ii) a capital divided into units that investors can <b>enter</b> and that can be <b>sold</b> at exit, and (iii) separation of <b>ownership and management</b> so investors don't have to run the business. A personalistic partnership (general or limited) fits none of this.

*Key points:* Capital company · Limited liability · Entry of investors / exit · Partnership doesn't fit


### (2) Why is a capital company more advisable than a partnership? (María wants to protect her wealth)

<ul><li><b>Liability</b>: in a general partnership partners answer for company debts <b>personally, unlimitedly, subsidiarily and jointly & severally</b> (arts. 127, 148 CCom). In an S.L./S.A. members are <b>not personally liable</b> (art. 1 LSC) – María risks only her contribution.</li><li><b>Transferability</b>: partnership status can't be transferred without the others' consent (art. 143 CCom); shares/stakes can be transferred (S.A. freely, S.L. with restrictions) → needed for the sale.</li><li><b>Management</b> is separated from ownership (directors), so investors can join without managing.</li><li>Capital companies are the standard vehicle for investors; partnerships are rarely used today.</li></ul>
  <p class="tip">But note: limited liability only works after <b>registration</b> (otherwise irregular company) and if they don't abuse the company (<b>veil lifting</b>).</p>

*Key points:* Art. 1 LSC – no personal liability · Partnership: personal, unlimited, subsidiary, joint & several · Transfer of status · Registration needed


### (3) S.L. or S.A.? Why?

<b>Recommendation: S.L. now.</b><ul><li>Minimum capital <b>€3,000</b> (S.A. €60,000), 100% paid but small.</li><li>No independent expert for non-cash contributions; fewer formalities, cheaper.</li><li><b>Flexible by-laws</b> (hybrid): founders can keep control (e.g. vote allocation, transfer restrictions, pre-emptive rights) while bringing in Business Angels.</li></ul>An <b>S.A.</b> becomes attractive if they need many investors, <b>free transferability</b> of shares or a <b>stock-market listing</b> (only S.A. can list). The S.L. can later be <b>transformed</b> into an S.A. (structural change).

*Key points:* €3,000 vs €60,000 · Fewer formalities · Control through flexible by-laws · Transform later / listing → S.A.


### (4) Main steps to set up the S.L.? Capital amount? Proportions? Can Pedro contribute his work? Can they invest through a company?

<b>Steps</b>: 1. Negative <b>name certificate</b> from the Central Mercantile Registry · 2. Open a <b>bank account</b> in the name of the company in formation and deposit the capital (bank certificate) · 3. Obtain a provisional <b>NIF</b> · 4. Draft the <b>by-laws</b> (name, purpose, office, capital, stakes, management body…) · 5. Execute the <b>deed of incorporation before a notary</b> · 6. Tax formalities · 7. <b>Register</b> in the Mercantile Registry of the province (max 1 year) → legal personality; definitive NIF; tax/social security registrations.<br>
  <b>Capital</b>: at least <b>€3,000</b> (in the notes); in practice enough to fund the first phase and look credible to investors.<br>
  <b>Proportions</b>: free. A 50/50 split is common but risks <b>deadlock = paralysis of corporate bodies</b>, a legal cause of dissolution → if 50/50, add a deadlock clause in a shareholders' agreement (or e.g. 51/49).<br>
  <b>Pedro's work</b>: <b>cannot</b> be a capital contribution (art. 58.2 LSC). He can commit to work as an <b>ancillary commitment</b> (art. 86 LSC), free or paid, outside capital.<br>
  <b>Through a company</b>: <b>yes</b> – legal persons can be founders/members (e.g. each founder's own holding S.L.).

*Key points:* Notary + Registry · €3,000 · 50/50 → paralysis risk · Work = ancillary commitment (art. 58.2, 86) · Holding company possible


### (5) What is a shareholders' agreement, its content, should she sign it, and when?

A <b>shareholders' agreement</b> (pacto de socios / parasocial) is a <b>private contract</b> between some or all shareholders regulating their relationship <b>beyond the by-laws</b>. It is <b>confidential</b>, not registered, and binds <b>only the signatories</b> (not the company or third parties) – that's why key points are often mirrored in the by-laws where possible.<br>
  <b>Typical content</b>: governance (board seats, <b>reserved matters / veto rights</b>, information rights) · <b>transfer restrictions</b>: lock-up, right of first refusal, <b>tag-along</b>, <b>drag-along</b> · <b>anti-dilution</b> & pre-emption · founders' dedication, <b>non-compete</b>, IP assignment · <b>vesting</b>, good/bad leaver · <b>deadlock</b> resolution · dividend policy · exit/sale provisions.<br>
  <b>Recommendation</b>: yes – sign one <b>at incorporation</b> between the founders (dedication, leaver, deadlock), and <b>amend/replace</b> it when investors enter (they will demand their own terms).

*Key points:* Private contract, binds signatories · Governance & vetoes · Transfer clauses (ROFR, tag, drag) · Sign at incorporation + update with investors


## Case 2 · Patinetes Eléctricos S.L. (Units 4–7)

**Facts.** <b>Facts 1</b>: after 2 years of losses, <b>equity €30,000</b> vs <b>share capital €90,000</b>. Pedro and María are <b>joint directors</b>.<br><b>Facts 2</b>: a <b>Business Angel</b> will invest <b>€100,000</b>; founders want to give <b>max 10%</b>. Capital €90,000 in <b>€1 par</b> stakes.<br><b>Facts 3</b>: subsidiary <b>PTEL S.L.U.</b> (100% owned, 5 employees, only serves the parent, no significant assets). Patinetes has 40 employees, all contracts, IP and licences. They will <b>merge</b>.


### 1. Could Pedro and María be held liable for the capital impairment – as directors? as shareholders? What should they do?

Equity €30,000 = <b>33% of capital</b> &lt; <b>½</b> (€45,000) → <b>legal cause of dissolution</b> (art. 363.1.e LSC) unless capital is increased or reduced sufficiently (and provided insolvency isn't due). The 2/3 mandatory reduction rule is only for S.A.<br>
  <b>As directors</b>: they must <b>convene the GM</b> (within <b>2 months</b>) to dissolve or adopt measures that fix the situation; if the GM doesn't agree, request <b>judicial dissolution</b>. Otherwise → <b>liability for company debts arising after the cause</b> (subsidiary, joint & several). If the company is <b>insolvent</b>, they must file within <b>2 months</b> or be liable for later debts (culpable insolvency).<br>
  <b>As shareholders</b>: <b>no</b> personal liability (capital company, art. 1 LSC).

*Key points:* 33% < 50% → dissolution cause · Convene GM (2 months) · Liable for later debts · Not as shareholders


### 2. Other management systems in S.L. and S.A.? Would liability change on a board?

Four models (art. 210 LSC): <b>sole director</b>; <b>joint & several</b> (each acts alone); <b>joint</b> (act together; S.A. max 2); <b>board</b> (min 3; S.L. max 12; collegiate). <br>On a board the regime is the <b>same</b>: all members who adopted or executed the harmful act/omission are <b>jointly & severally liable</b> (art. 237), unless they prove they <b>expressly opposed</b>, <b>didn't know</b>, or did <b>everything possible</b> to avoid the damage. As joint directors both must act together, so both are equally responsible.

*Key points:* 4 models · Art. 237 joint & several · Exoneration: opposed / unaware / did everything


### 3. Measures to reverse the capital impairment?

Goal: equity ≥ ½ capital.<ul><li><b>Capital increase</b> with new money – the BA's €100,000 (cash) → equity 130,000 vs capital 100,000 ✔.</li><li><b>Increase by offsetting loans</b> (shareholder loans turned into capital – S.L.: 100% liquid, mature, payable).</li><li><b>Capital reduction to compensate losses</b> (S.L. capital stays ≥ €3,000): e.g. reduce capital to €50,000 → equity 30,000 ≥ 25,000 ✔.</li><li><b>Accordion</b>: reduction + simultaneous increase.</li><li>Members' contributions to equity / return to profit.</li></ul>

*Key points:* Cash increase (BA) · Loans → capital · Reduction for losses · Accordion


### 4. Main legal obligations of directors? Liabilities if breached?

<b>Obligations</b>: act within law, by-laws and GM instructions; <b>duty of care</b> (orderly entrepreneur: prudence, competence, dedication, information, vigilance – incl. risk monitoring); <b>duty of loyalty</b> (no conflicts, secrecy, no competition); prepare <b>annual accounts</b>; convene the <b>ordinary GM</b> yearly and extraordinary GMs when required (dissolution causes); request <b>insolvency</b> on time.<br><b>Liabilities</b>: for <b>debts</b> (insolvency not filed / dissolution GM not convened → later debts) and for <b>damages</b> (art. 236: fault + damage + causal link; GM approval doesn't exonerate) via the <b>corporate</b> or <b>individual</b> action; plus <b>tax, labour and criminal</b> liability.

*Key points:* Care + loyalty · Accounts, GM, insolvency · Debts vs damages · Corporate & individual actions


### 5 (i–iii). Type of capital increase? Other contributions? S.L. vs S.A.? Steps? Pre-emptive right?

<b>Type</b>: increase with <b>cash contributions</b>, creating <b>new stakes</b> with a <b>share premium</b>. Others: <b>non-cash</b>, <b>offsetting loans</b>, <b>reserves</b>. Differences: S.A. needs an <b>expert report</b> for non-cash and <b>auditor's certificate</b> for loans (≥25% due, rest ≤5 years) and may <b>delegate</b> the increase to directors; S.L. needs 100% of loans due.<br>
  <b>Steps</b>: directors' proposal/report → GM convened (15 days, agenda + full text) → GM resolution (S.L.: <b>&gt; ½ of all votes</b>) → handling the pre-emptive right → subscription & payment (bank certificate) → <b>public deed</b> → <b>registration</b> of resolution + execution together.<br>
  <b>Pre-emptive right</b>: <b>yes</b>, Pedro and María have it (cash increase, ≥ 1 month). To let the BA in, they <b>waive it individually</b> (simplest, e.g. in a universal meeting) or the GM <b>excludes</b> it.

*Key points:* Cash + new stakes + premium · Steps: report → GM → subscription → deed → registry · Pre-emptive right → waiver


### 5 (iv–vi). Pre-money valuation, anti-dilution mechanism and the figures of the resolution

<div class="dia calc"><div>10% for €100,000 → post-money <b>€1,000,000</b> → <b>pre-money €900,000</b></div><div>Per existing stake: 900,000 ÷ 90,000 = <b>€10</b></div><div>New stakes N: N ÷ (90,000 + N) = 10% → <b>N = 10,000</b></div><div>Premium per stake = 10 − 1 = <b>€9</b> → total premium <b>€90,000</b></div></div>
  <b>Mechanism</b>: the <b>share premium</b> – the BA pays the real value (€10) instead of par (€1), so the founders' stakes aren't diluted in value.<br><br>
  <i>"To increase the corporate capital of the Company, currently € 90,000, by € <b>10,000</b>, by creating <b>10,000</b> new shares, numbered from 90,001 to <b>100,000</b>, both inclusive. Consequently, the number of shares shall be <b>100,000</b>, with a face value of € 1 each. The new shares are created with a total issue premium of € <b>90,000</b>, resulting in an issue premium of € <b>9</b> per share. Therefore, the issued share capital and issue premium total € <b>100,000</b>. Consequently, the corporate capital shall be € <b>100,000</b>, represented by <b>100,000</b> shares, with a face value of € 1 each, numbered from 1 to <b>100,000</b>, both inclusive."</i>

*Key points:* Pre-money €900,000 · 10,000 new stakes · €9 premium / €90,000 · Capital €100,000


### 6. Which company should absorb which?

<b>PATINETES ELÉCTRICOS, S.L. absorbs PTEL, S.L.U.</b> Patinetes holds the 40 employees, all client and supplier contracts, the IP (trademarks, patents, domains) and the <b>licences and permits</b>. If PTEL absorbed Patinetes, all of these would have to pass to PTEL by universal succession – administrative licences may not be transferable, contracts may contain change-of-control clauses, and registrations would have to be updated. Absorbing the small subsidiary moves almost nothing (5 employees, no significant assets).

*Key points:* Patinetes = absorbing · Licences/contracts/IP stay put · PTEL extinguished


### 7. Type of merger and requirements avoided?

<b>Merger by absorption of a wholly-owned subsidiary</b> – a <b>special merger</b> (art. 53 RDLME). Avoided: <b>exchange ratio</b> (no new shares to PTEL's member – it's Patinetes itself), <b>capital increase</b> of the absorbing company, <b>directors' report</b> and <b>independent expert report</b>, and <b>approval by the GM of the absorbed company</b>. A simplified common draft is enough.

*Key points:* Absorption of 100% subsidiary · Art. 53 RDLME · No exchange ratio / capital increase / reports / absorbed GM


### 8. Procedure in chronological order

<ol><li>Directors of both companies draw up and sign the (simplified) <b>common draft terms of merger</b>.</li><li><b>Publication/deposit</b> of the draft in the Registry or on the websites, at least <b>1 month</b> before the GM (or individual notice; unanimous universal GM avoids it).</li><li><b>Merger balance sheets</b> (≤ 6 months; audited if required).</li><li><b>GM of Patinetes approves</b> the merger within <b>6 months</b> of the draft (PTEL's GM not needed).</li><li><b>Publication</b> of the resolution in the BORME + a newspaper, or individual written notice to members and creditors.</li><li><b>1-month creditors' opposition</b> period.</li><li><b>Public deed</b> of merger before a notary.</li><li><b>Registration</b> in the Mercantile Registry → merger effective, PTEL <b>extinguished</b> and its entries cancelled.</li></ol>

*Key points:* Draft → publish → balance → GM → publish → 1 month → deed → registry


### 9. When is the merger effective legally and for accounting?

<b>Legally</b>: on <b>registration</b> of the merger deed in the Mercantile Registry (universal succession, extinction of PTEL).<br><b>Accounting</b>: from the date stated in the merger draft from which PTEL's operations are considered carried out on behalf of Patinetes – it can be <b>earlier</b> than the legal effect.

*Key points:* Legal: registration · Accounting: date in the draft (retroactive)


### 10. Reasons for the merger? Should they be recorded in the merger project?

<b>Reasons</b>: PTEL lost its purpose (only serves the parent) → <b>simplify</b> the group, <b>cut costs</b> (one set of accounts, audits, tax filings, governance), integrate the marketing team, synergies, single brand/contract holder.<br><b>Recording them</b>: not a mandatory item of the simplified draft, but <b>convenient</b>: it explains the economic reasons to members and creditors (transparency) and documents <b>valid economic reasons</b>, relevant e.g. for the tax-neutral merger regime.

*Key points:* Simplification & cost savings · Transparency · Valid economic reasons (tax)
