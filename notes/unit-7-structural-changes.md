## 7.1 Structural changes

**Q: Which law regulates **structural changes**?**

**Real Decreto-ley 5/2023, of 28 June (RDLME)** – applies to all commercial companies (and partnerships).


**Q: Closed list of structural changes?**

1. **Transformation**
2. **Merger**
3. **Division** (demerger)
4. **Global assignment of assets and liabilities**
(+ international transfer of registered office) – domestic and cross-border.


**Q: Three phases of the merger procedure?**

1. **Preparation**
2. **Approval**
3. **Execution**



## 7.2 Mergers

**Q: Legal definition of a **merger**?**

A corporate operation affecting **two or more companies** that leads to the **extinction of all or some** of them and the **integration of their assets and members** into a **single company**.


**Q: Two types of merger?**

1. **By creation of a new company** – **all** merging companies are extinguished; a new one acquires everything by universal succession
2. **By absorption** – one company **absorbs** the others, which are dissolved and extinguished (absorber usually **increases capital**)

> Mock Q18(d) merger by absorption is a distractor – the block transfer where the transferring company receives shares is **segregation**.


**Q: Typical economic reasons for mergers?**

**Synergies, economies of scale, vertical integration, optimised financing**, growth in size.


**Q: What is the **common draft terms of merger** (art. 39 RDLME) and its timing?**

Drawn up by the **managing bodies of all companies**; published in the **Registry or on each website** at least **1 month** before the GMs. Must be approved within **6 months** of signature, otherwise it **lapses**.


**Q: Which reports accompany the merger draft?**

A **directors' report** of each company justifying it + an **independent expert** report – **mandatory when any company is an S.A.**


**Q: What is the **exchange ratio** (exchange equation)?**

The number of shares/stakes in the absorbing/new company given to members of the extinguished companies **in proportion to their participation**, calculated at **fair value** – so the value of members' equity is **not reduced**.


**Q: Approval phase of a merger?**

**GM of every company** approves the **common draft** strictly (art. 47), with LSC quorums/majorities. Notice **≥ 1 month** before; **merger balance sheet** ≤ **6 months** old (audited if required). **Consent** needed from members who become **unlimitedly liable**.


**Q: Can merger formalities be simplified by unanimity?**

Yes: if approved at a **universal GM by unanimity** in **each** company → no prior publication/deposit and **no directors' report** needed.


**Q: Execution phase of a merger? When is it legally effective?**

After the **1-month creditor opposition** period → **public deed** → **registration** in the Mercantile Registry. The merger takes legal effect **upon registration**. Afterwards it can only be challenged for breach of the RDLME, within **3 months**.


**Q: What are **special mergers** (art. 53 ff. RDLME)?**

Simplified mergers where the **same persons decide** in all companies. Main case: **absorption of a wholly-owned (100%) subsidiary** – simplified draft, **no expert reports**, **no capital increase** of the absorber, **no GM approval** of the absorbed company.

> This is exactly **Case 2, Facts 3** (PATINETES absorbs PTEL S.L.U.).



## 7.3 Divisions

**Q: Why divide a company?**

To **reorganise**: separate activities or production phases into different companies, or distribute assets according to different financial needs. The opposite of a merger; merger rules apply (art. 63).


**Q: **Total demerger** (split-up), art. 59?**

The company is **extinguished**; all its assets are divided into **two or more blocks**, each transferred to a new or existing company; the **members** receive shares of the beneficiaries in proportion.

> Mock Q4 → (d): members of the divided company become members of the beneficiaries and the divided company is **extinguished**.


**Q: **Partial demerger** (spin-off), art. 60?**

Block transfer of **one or more parts** of the assets to beneficiaries; the **members** receive their shares; the divided company **survives** but **reduces its capital**.


**Q: **Segregation**, art. 61?**

Block transfer of one or more parts of the assets to beneficiaries, but the **transferring company itself** receives the shares → **parent–subsidiary** relationship.

> Mock Q18 → (a) segregation. Mock Q4(b) is wrong: in segregation members do **not** become direct owners of the beneficiaries' shares.


**Q: Quick test: who receives the beneficiary's shares, and does the original company survive?**

- **Total demerger**: members · company **extinguished**
- **Partial demerger**: members · company **survives** (capital ↓)
- **Segregation**: **the company itself** · company **survives**



## 7.4 Dissolution, liquidation, extinction

**Q: Three phases of company termination?**

1. **Dissolution** (declaratory act)
2. **Liquidation**
3. **Extinction** (termination)

> The company does **not** end in a single act. Mock Q19(d) → false.


**Q: Causes of dissolution (art. 363 LSC + voluntary)?**

1. **Term** in by-laws expires
2. **GM resolution**
3. **Cessation** of the activity / **impossibility** of the corporate purpose
4. **Paralysis** of corporate bodies
5. **Losses** → equity **< ½ of capital** (unless capital increased/reduced enough, and if insolvency isn't due)
6. Capital **below the legal minimum**

> Mock Q19 → (b): the law sets **legal causes** of mandatory dissolution.


**Q: Example of **paralysis of corporate bodies**?**

Two members with **50% each** who always vote opposite → no resolutions possible in GM or managing body → legal cause of dissolution.

> Very relevant for Case 1 (Pedro & María 50/50!) → use a shareholders' agreement with a deadlock clause.


**Q: Who declares dissolution and what if the GM doesn't?**

The **GM**. With a legal cause, directors **must convene** it (any member may request it, art. 365.1). If the GM rejects or can't agree → directors must request **judicial dissolution**. Failure → directors liable for debts arising **after** the cause. Formalised in **public deed + Registry** (or court judgment).


**Q: What happens in **liquidation**?**

The company **keeps legal personality**, adds "**en liquidación**" to its name, stops its corporate purpose. **Directors cease**; **liquidators** take over (art. 375). Tasks: **inventory & balance** within **3 months**, **pay creditors**, final balance, **distribute** the remainder to members in proportion to their capital.


**Q: When is the company **extinguished**?**

When, after liquidation and distribution, the liquidators grant a **public deed of extinction** and its entries are **cancelled** in the Registry; books are deposited there. → **End of legal personality**.


**Q: Can dissolution, liquidation and extinction happen in one act?**

Yes – if there are **no assets or liabilities** left, a deed of **dissolution and extinction** can be granted in the same act (initial balance = final balance).
