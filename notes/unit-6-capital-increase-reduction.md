# Unit 6 – Amendments to the By-laws: Capital Increase & Reduction

## 6.1 Amending the by-laws

**Q: Who amends the by-laws?**

As a rule **only the general meeting** (art. 285.1 LSC). The GM may amend freely unless unlawful or against the principles of the company type (art. 28).


**Q: Requirements for a by-law amendment?**

1. Points clearly stated in the **notice/agenda**
2. Full **text** of the proposal (+ **written report** in the **S.A.**, art. 286)
3. Notice states members' right to **examine/receive** the documents free
4. Adopted with legal **quorum & majorities** (arts. 194, 201)
5. **Public deed** + **Registry**


**Q: Who can move the **registered office**?**

**Within Spain**: the **directors** (unless the by-laws expressly deny it) – art. **285.2 LSC**. **Abroad**: GM (structural change, RDL 5/2023).

> Mock Q17(d) "GM has no power to approve a transfer within Spain, exclusive competence of directors" → **false**. Correct: (c).


**Q: When does an amendment need the **consent of the affected member**?**

When it creates **new obligations** for members (e.g. payments to compensate losses, ancillary commitments) or, in the **S.A.**, restricts the **transfer** of registered shares or affects **classes** of shares (art. 291 ff.).


**Q: What is the **right to exit** (derecho de separación) and when for a corporate purpose change?**

The right to **leave** the company and get the **fair value** of the shares/stakes. Granted when the GM **substitutes or substantially modifies the corporate purpose** (art. **346 LSC**) – the nature of the investment changes.



## 6.2 Capital increase

**Q: Two **ways** to increase capital?**

1. **Issue new shares / create new stakes**
2. **Increase the par value** of existing ones (art. 295.1 LSC)


**Q: Four types of capital increase by **exchange value** (contravalor)?**

1. **Cash** contributions
2. **Non-cash** contributions
3. **Offsetting loans** (credit compensation)
4. Charged to **profits or reserves**

> Mind map: **Cash, Non-cash, Loans, Reserves**.


**Q: Who decides a capital increase? Can it be delegated?**

The **GM** (by-law amendment). In the **S.A.**, the GM may **delegate** to the directors within limits (art. 297 LSC).


**Q: Three general steps of a capital increase?**

1. **Resolution** of the GM (directors' report, art. 286)
2. **Execution** – subscription/take-up and payment
3. **Registration** of resolution **and** execution **simultaneously** (art. 315)


**Q: Why must resolution and execution be registered **together**?**

So the Registry publishes only increases that **actually happened** (who subscribed, how it was paid) – otherwise capital would lose credibility as a solvency indicator.


**Q: Effect of a **cash** increase on equity and liquidity?**

**+ cash → + capital → + equity**. More immediate liquidity and better long-term solvency. Need a **bank certificate**.


**Q: Effect of a **non-cash** increase? Extra requirement in S.A.?**

**+ net assets → + capital → + equity**; liquidity not necessarily. **S.A.**: **independent expert report** (Registrar); not required in S.L.


**Q: Capital increase by **offsetting loans**: effect and requirements?**

**− debt → + capital → + equity** (no new cash). **S.A.**: ≥ **25%** of the loans **liquid, mature and payable** and the rest due within **5 years** + **auditor's certificate**. **S.L.**: **100%** liquid, mature and payable (art. 301).

> Mock Q20 → (c): increase by offsetting loans **does** increase equity.


**Q: Capital increase charged to **reserves**: effect on equity?**

**Equity unchanged** – just a reclassification (reserves → capital). Requires a balance sheet (audited if mandatory) approved **≤ 6 months** before (art. 303). Effect: reserves become **unavailable** capital → stronger solvency image.

> Mock Q20(a) "always increases equity" → false; (d) "increases equity if made by reserves" → false.


**Q: What is a **share premium** (prima de emisión) and why use it?**

Paying **more than par value** for new shares/stakes (art. 298), fully paid on subscription. Used when **new members** enter: compensates the old members for **reserves and expectations** built before → avoids **dilution of value**.



## 6.3 Pre-emptive right

**Q: What is the **pre-emptive right**? In which increases?**

The old members' right to subscribe/take up new shares/stakes **in proportion** to their par value **before third parties** – in increases with **cash contributions** (art. 304 LSC). Called "preferential subscription right" in S.A.

> Purpose: keep their **relative share** (% of capital).


**Q: Period to exercise the pre-emptive right?**

Set by the directors, **not less than one month** (different for listed companies).


**Q: What do members risk if they don't exercise the pre-emptive right?**

Lower % → **smaller dividends**; drop in **book value** per share (unless a premium is charged); and they may fall **below 5%**, losing minority rights (e.g. requesting a GM, liability action).


**Q: How can the pre-emptive right be removed?**

The **GM** may **exclude** it fully/partly (with specific requirements), or each member may **individually waive** it.



## 6.4 Capital reduction

**Q: Three ways to reduce capital (art. 317.2)?**

1. **Reduce par value**
2. **Redeem** (amortise) shares/stakes
3. **Group** them to exchange them


**Q: Purposes of a capital reduction (art. 317.1)?**

1. **Restore balance** between capital and equity reduced by **losses**
2. Create/increase **legal or voluntary reserves**
3. **Refund** contributions to members
4. S.A.: **release** members from **called-up capital**

> Mind map: balance restoration, reserve creation, refund contributions. Forms: voluntary, mandatory, **accordion**.


**Q: When is a capital reduction **mandatory**?**

Only in the **S.A.**: when losses reduce **equity below 2/3 of capital** and it isn't recovered within **one fiscal year** (art. **327 LSC**). It must affect all shares proportionally (art. 320).

> Don't mix up: **2/3 + 1 year → mandatory reduction (S.A.)** vs **½ → mandatory dissolution cause (S.A. and S.L.)**.


**Q: Annex: S.A. capital €100,000, losses €50,000. Equity? Reduction of €30,000 – result?**

Equity = €50,000 = 50% < 2/3 → after 1 year reduction mandatory. Reduce by €30,000: capital **€70,000**, losses **€20,000**, equity still **€50,000** = **71%** of capital > 2/3 ✔.
Book entry: €30,000 (Capital) to (Accumulated losses) €30,000 – **no money moves**.


**Q: Steps of a capital reduction?**

1. **GM resolution** (by-law amendment; directors' report justifying reason, amount, procedure…)
2. **Execution**
3. **Creditors' right to oppose** (S.A., certain cases)
4. **Registration**


**Q: When do creditors have a **right to oppose** a capital reduction?**

In the **S.A.**, when the reduction **really reduces equity** (refund of contributions, release from called-up capital). **Not** when it restores the balance after losses or funds reserves (art. 335). Exercise within **1 month** of the last announcement; the reduction can't be executed until the debt is **secured** (e.g. bank guarantee).

> Mock Q15 → (a). (c) "always granted" → false; (b) "directors decide" → false.


**Q: What is the **"accordion"** operation (art. 343 LSC)?**

A **reduction to compensate losses below the legal minimum** combined with a **simultaneous increase**. Conditions: (a) increased to **≥ legal minimum**, (b) **pre-emptive right** respected, (c) reduction **conditional** on the increase being executed, (d) **registered together**.

> Alternative to avoid dissolution: **transform** the S.A. into an **S.L.** (min €3,000).
