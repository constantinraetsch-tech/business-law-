## 8.1 Intellectual property

**Q: Two categories of **intellectual property** law?**

1. **Copyright** – literary/artistic works, software, films, performers
2. **Industrial property** – creations with **industrial application**: **distinctive signs** (trademarks, trade names) and **industrial creations** (patents, utility models, industrial designs)


**Q: Double economic function of industrial property law?**

1. **Private interest**: reward and incentivise the author's effort
2. **General interest**: distinctive signs → **market transparency**; patents → inventions are **disclosed** and help progress


**Q: How does the law protect industrial property?**

By granting the author an **exclusive right of use**, which means **others are prohibited** from using it unless authorised.


**Q: Which laws regulate trademarks and patents in Spain?**

**Trademarks Act 17/2001** (LM) for trademarks & trade names; **Patents Act 24/2015** (LP) for patents & utility models.



## 8.2 Trademarks

**Q: Define a **trademark** (art. 4.1 LM).**

**Any sign** (words, names, drawings, letters, numbers, colours, shape/packaging, sounds…) **capable of distinguishing the products or services** of one company from those of others.

> Essential element: **distinctiveness**.


**Q: Trademark vs **trade name** vs company name?**

**Trademark** → distinguishes **products/services** (Zara, Bershka).
**Trade name** (art. 87 LM) → identifies **the business/entrepreneur** in trade (GRUPO INDITEX).
**Company name** → legal name (Industria de Diseño Textil, S.A.).
A trade name does **not** let you mark products – you need a trademark.


**Q: Are **shop signs** (rótulos) protected by the Trademarks Act?**

**No.** Using a shop sign to the detriment of an earlier one can be **unfair competition** (misleading act).


**Q: What is the **principle of specialty**?**

A trademark is registered for specific **classes** of goods/services (**Nice Classification**) and protected only within them – except **renowned** trademarks.


**Q: Five types of trademarks?**

1. **Collective** – members of an **association** (owner = association), e.g. Barcelona Bar
2. **Guarantee/warranty** – certifies common **quality/requirements** (e.g. CE marking)
3. **Known / renowned** (art. 8) – generally known; protected **even unregistered** and **beyond its class**
4. **EU trademark** – Reg. 2017/1001, whole EU
5. **International** – protected in Spain on request (art. 79)


**Q: How are **renowned** trademarks protected? Example?**

Owner can prohibit use **even for other classes** (and protection even without registration in Spain). **ZARA vs "Zara Tanzania Adventures"**: EUIPO let Inditex win against a travel company.


**Q: How is the **right to a trademark** acquired?**

By **valid registration** at the **OEPM** (Spanish Patent & Trademark Office) – **constitutive** effect. After a **2-month opposition period**, the grant is published in the official gazette.


**Q: Content of the trademark right (art. 34 LM)?**

1. **Exclusive use** in trade
2. **Prohibit** others from using identical/similar signs for the same/similar goods
3. **Legal standing** to oppose registrations and seek **nullity** of confusing later marks


**Q: Duration of a trademark?**

**10 years from application**, **renewable indefinitely** for 10-year periods (art. 31).

> Trademark can live forever; patent can't (20 years, no renewal).


**Q: Obligation to use a trademark?**

**Effective and real use in Spain**. Not used for **5 years** after registration (or **5 uninterrupted years**) → **lapse (caducidad)**, registration cancelled (art. 39).


**Q: Can a trademark be transferred or licensed?**

Yes – it's an **intangible asset** (value = prestige + customer loyalty). **Licence** (art. 48): licensor authorises licensee for a **royalty**; for all/part of Spain, all/some classes, **exclusive or non-exclusive**.


**Q: **Absolute** vs **relative** prohibitions – the key difference for nullity actions?**

**Absolute** (art. 5): sign itself unfit / contrary to public order → nullity action **imprescriptible** (no time limit).
**Relative** (art. 6 ff.): conflict with **earlier rights of others** → nullity action within **5 years**.


**Q: Main **absolute** prohibitions (art. 5 LM)?**

1. Not a valid sign (art. 4)
2. **Devoid of distinctive character**
3. Exclusively **descriptive** (kind, quality, geographical origin…)
4. Exclusively **customary** in current language/trade
5. Contrary to **public policy/morality**
6. Reproduce **flags/emblems** of Spain, regions, towns without consent
7. **Deceptive** (nature, quality, geographical origin)

> Examples from class: "ECO PURE", "BioFresh 100%", "Barcelona Tapas" → descriptive/deceptive (absolute).


**Q: Main **relative** prohibitions?**

Signs **confusable** with **earlier registered** trademarks/trade names for identical/similar goods, or taking **unfair advantage** of a **renowned** mark.

> Class examples: "Coca-Kola", "GOOGLE for Kids" → relative. Apple Corps vs Apple Computer (iTunes).


**Q: Define a **trade name** (art. 87 LM). What can it consist of?**

Any sign capable of graphic representation that **identifies a business** in trade and distinguishes it from businesses with identical/similar activities. Can be: personal names, company names, **imaginary** names, names alluding to the activity, anagrams, logos, images… Need not equal the company name. Specialty applies; **trademark rules apply** where compatible.



## 8.3 Industrial creations & patents

**Q: What is an **industrial creation**? Invention vs discovery?**

The result of human activity offering a **new technical solution** to a problem or improving an industrial process. A **discovery** doesn't necessarily have direct industrial application → **not** an invention.


**Q: Types of industrial creations?**

a) **Inventions** (patents) – substantive
b) **Utility models** – configuration with a practical advantage
c) **Industrial designs** – aesthetic/ornamental appearance


**Q: Why does the law grant an exclusive right, if it restricts free competition?**

To protect a **higher good**: without reward, less inventive effort. A **temporary** monopoly encourages **technological progress**; afterwards the invention enters the **public domain**.


**Q: What is a **patent**?**

A **title** granted by the State recognising an **invention capable of industrial application** and the rights derived from it – the inventor's **exclusive** right to exploit it.


**Q: Three requirements for patentability (art. 4.1 LP)?**

1. **Novelty**
2. **Inventive step**
3. **Industrial application**

> Memory trick **N-I-I**: "**N**ew **I**dea, **I**ndustrial".


**Q: When is an invention **new** (art. 6)?**

When it's not part of the **state of the art** = everything made **available to the public in Spain or abroad** (written, oral, use…) before the filing date. **Absolute, worldwide** novelty.


**Q: When is there an **inventive step** (art. 8)?**

When it **doesn't follow obviously** from the state of the art for a **person skilled** in the field.


**Q: When is it capable of **industrial application** (art. 9)?**

When it can be manufactured or used in **any kind of industry, including agriculture**.


**Q: What is **not** considered an invention (art. 4)?**

**Discoveries, scientific theories, mathematical methods**; literary/artistic/aesthetic works; schemes/rules/methods for mental acts, games or **business**; **computer programs**; presentations of information. (May be protected otherwise, e.g. copyright.)


**Q: Inventions **excluded** from patents even if they meet the requirements (art. 5)?**

Contrary to **public order/morality**; **plant varieties and animal breeds**; essentially biological processes; **surgical/therapeutic/diagnostic methods** on humans/animals; **human cloning**, commercial use of human embryos.


**Q: Who owns the right to a patent?**

The **inventor** (or heirs/successors); several inventors → **in common**. If a non-entitled person applies → action before the OEPM (before grant) or **claim of ownership** in court (after grant), arts. 11–12.


**Q: Employee inventions – three types (art. 15 ff.)?**

1. **Service/employer** inventions – research is the **object of the contract** → belong to the **employer**
2. **Mixed** – employee not hired to research, but used **company knowledge/resources** → employer **can claim** ownership or use, **paying compensation**
3. **Free** – otherwise → belong to the **employee**

> EcoTech case (researcher invents at home with company lab equipment): he *is* a researcher → if it falls within his research duties = **service** invention; if outside them but using company equipment = **mixed**.


**Q: Content of the patent right?**

**Exclusive right to exploit** + prevent others from manufacturing, offering, introducing on the market or using; for **process patents** also covers the **products directly obtained**.


**Q: Duration and territory of a patent?**

**20 years, NOT renewable**, limited to **Spanish territory**. Then → public domain.


**Q: Obligation to **exploit** a patent?**

Exploit (directly or via licensee) within **3 years from publication of the grant** or **4 years from filing**, enough to supply the Spanish market (art. 90). Otherwise → **compulsory licence**.

> Content guide shorthand: "loss due to no exploitation (3 years)".


**Q: When can a **compulsory licence** be imposed (art. 91)?**

**Lack or insufficient exploitation**; **public interest** (public health, national defence); to stop practices contrary to **competition law**.
