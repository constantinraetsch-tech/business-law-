# Unit 4 – Corporate Bodies I: General Meeting & Directors

24 flashcards · same content as the app and the RECALL decks.


## 4.1 Two corporate bodies

**Q: What are the two **corporate bodies** of a capital company?**

1. **General meeting** (junta general) – forms the **will of the members**
2. **Managing body** (órgano de administración, "directors") – **manages and represents** the company


**Q: Can members, as members, represent the company?**

**No.** Members **choose the directors** who represent and manage it. Members sit higher in hierarchy but have **no power of representation**.


**Q: Must directors be members?**

**No** – unless the by-laws require it (art. **212.2 LSC**). Principle of **separation** between ownership (members) and management (directors). One person can be both, wearing two hats.



## 4.2 General meeting

**Q: Define the **general meeting**. Who is bound by its resolutions?**

A **meeting of members** that adopts decisions **by majority** on matters within its competence. **All members** are bound – including **dissenting and absent** ones (they may challenge).


**Q: Can a 70% member take GM decisions alone without a meeting?**

**No** – formalities must be met to **protect minorities**. Only exception: a **single-member company** (the sole member exercises the GM's powers).


**Q: Competences of the general meeting (art. 160 LSC)?**

a) **Annual accounts**, distribution of profit, approval of management
b) **Appoint/remove directors**, liquidators, auditors; **liability action** against them
c) **Amend the by-laws**
d) **Capital increase/reduction**
e) **Remove/limit pre-emptive rights**
f) Acquire/dispose/contribute **core assets** (> **25%** of total assets)
g) **Transformation, merger, division, global assignment**, transfer of office **abroad**
h) **Dissolution**
i) **Final liquidation balance**
j) Others set by law/by-laws

> Mnemonic: **A-D-B-C-P-C-S-D-L** → "**A**ll **D**irectors **B**ring **C**offee, **P**artners **C**hoose **S**ugar, **D**ON'T **L**eave". It's a **closed list** – the GM can't decide outside it.


**Q: When is an asset a **core asset** requiring GM approval?**

When the transaction amount exceeds **25% of the total assets** on the last approved balance sheet.


**Q: What is the **ordinary general meeting** and when must it be held (art. 164 LSC)?**

The meeting to approve **management**, **annual accounts** of the previous year and **distribution of profit**; must meet within the **first six months** of each fiscal year. Everything else → **extraordinary** GM.

> Mock Q13 → (d): the GM is ordinary or extraordinary **depending on the matters** dealt with.


**Q: Who **convenes** the GM and when must they?**

The **directors**:
- **Ordinary GM every year**
- When **appropriate** for the company
- When **law/by-laws** require
- When requested by members with **≥ 5% of capital**
- In case of a **mandatory dissolution** cause


**Q: How is a GM **announced** and with what notice period?**

On the **company website** (or, if none, in the **BORME** + a widely-read newspaper of the province); by-laws may provide **individual written notice** to each member. Notice period: **S.A. ≥ 1 month**, **S.L. ≥ 15 days**.


**Q: What is a **universal meeting** (art. 178 LSC)?**

A GM validly held **without prior notice** when **all the capital is present or represented** and attendees **unanimously** agree to hold it and on its agenda.

> Typical of companies with **very few members** – you can only know it is universal when everybody is actually there.


**Q: Three steps for valid GM decisions?**

1. **Convene** the members
2. **Constitute** the meeting (S.A.: minimum **quorum**)
3. **Hold** it and **vote** with the legal/statutory **majorities** (different S.A./S.L.)


**Q: Is a minimum **quorum** required to constitute the GM?**

**S.A.: yes** – % of capital present/represented; depends on the **matter** and on **first or second call** (arts. 193–194 LSC; stricter for by-law amendments, transformation, merger, division). **S.L.: no quorum.**

> S.A. quorum (LSC): ordinary matters **25%** 1st call / **any** 2nd call; special matters **50%** 1st / **25%** 2nd.


**Q: Can the GM decide on matters **not on the agenda**?**

Only two exceptions: **removal of directors** and bringing a **liability action** against them.


**Q: How are GM **majorities** regulated?**

Different regimes for **S.A.** (also depends on 1st/2nd call) and **S.L.**, and stricter majorities for important matters (arts. 198–201 LSC). Resolutions go into the **minutes book** legalised by the Registry.

> See the cheat-sheet table: S.L. ordinary = majority of votes cast ≥ **1/3** of all votes; by-law amendments/capital changes **> 1/2** of all votes; merger/transformation/exclusion of pre-emptive right **2/3**.


**Q: What can a member do if he disagrees with a GM resolution?**

He is **bound** by it, but may **challenge** it in court if it violates the **law, the by-laws** or harms the **company's interest** (art. 204 LSC).



## 4.3 Managing body

**Q: Define the **managing body**.**

The corporate body that **legally represents** the company and **manages** it under the law (art. **209 LSC**). Management isn't listed exhaustively – it covers all acts aimed at the **corporate purpose**.


**Q: Who **cannot** be a director?**

Non-emancipated **minors**, the legally **incompetent**, persons **disqualified** under the Insolvency Act, persons **convicted** of certain offences (art. 213 LSC), and those with **incompatible public posts** (art. 14 CCom: judges, magistrates, heads of government…). Directors can be **natural or legal persons**.


**Q: Can the GM interfere in management?**

As a rule **no** – directors manage autonomously; the GM appoints/removes them and **evaluates** management annually. Directors are personally liable even if the GM **authorised or ratified** the act.


**Q: Four ways of organising the managing body (art. 210 LSC)?**

1. **Sole director** (administrador único)
2. **Joint and several directors** (solidarios) – **each acts alone** with full powers
3. **Joint directors** (mancomunados) – must **act together**; **S.A.: max 2**
4. **Board of directors** (consejo) – collegiate, majority; **min 3**, S.L. **max 12**

> Mind map shorthand: **SD, JD, J&SD, BoD**. Listed companies must have a board (art. 529 bis).


**Q: Joint **and several** directors vs **joint** directors – who can sign alone?**

**Joint and several (solidarios)**: **each one alone**.
**Joint (mancomunados)**: **together** – none alone.

> Mock Q12(a) "directors acting severally and jointly → all have to act jointly" → **false**. Spanish: solidario = jeder allein; mancomunado = gemeinsam.


**Q: S.A. wants 3 directors acting **jointly**. Possible?**

**No** – S.A. may have at most **2 joint directors**; with more than two acting jointly it must be a **board of directors**.


**Q: How is the organisational model of the managing body changed?**

The by-laws choose one of the 4 models; any change → **public deed** + **registration** in the Mercantile Registry (art. 210.4 LSC).


**Q: Directors' term of office?**

**S.A.: max 6 years**, re-electable for periods of the same maximum length. **S.L.: indefinite** (unless the by-laws limit it). The GM can **remove** them at any time following formalities, even before the end of the term.

> Mock Q14: the **incorrect** statement is (b) – S.L. directors are **not "always"** indefinite "on a mandatory basis". Mock Q12 → (d) board acts collegiately.
