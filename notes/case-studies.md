## Case 1 – Setting up the start-up

**Q: Case 1: Pedro & María want to grow, attract Business Angels then VC, and sell. Which company type?**

A **capital company** – most likely an **S.L.** now (low capital, flexible, cheap), possibly **transformed into an S.A.** later (many investors, free transfer, listing).

> Growth + investors + exit ⇒ limited liability and a capital split into transferable units. A partnership doesn't fit (unlimited liability, no transfer without consent).


**Q: Case 1: why a capital company rather than a partnership (María wants to protect her wealth)?**

- Members **not personally liable** (art. 1 LSC) – María risks only her contribution
- Easier **entry of investors** and **transfer** of shares/stakes
- **Separation** of ownership/management (investors don't need to manage)
- Credibility, standard for VC

> In a general partnership she would be **personally, unlimitedly, jointly & severally** liable.


**Q: Case 1: S.L. or S.A.? Arguments.**

**S.L.**: min **€3,000**, 100% paid, **no expert report**, fewer formalities, flexible by-laws (vote allocation, transfer restrictions keep control).
**S.A.**: min **€60,000**, shares **freely transferable**, needed for **listing**, more formalities.
→ **S.L. now**, transform later if needed.


**Q: Case 1: main steps to set up the S.L.?**

1. **Name certificate** (Central Mercantile Registry)
2. **Bank account** + deposit capital → **bank certificate**
3. Provisional **NIF** (tax ID)
4. Draft **by-laws**
5. **Deed of incorporation before a notary**
6. Tax formalities
7. **Register** in the Mercantile Registry of the province (within 1 year) → legal personality; definitive NIF


**Q: Case 1: capital amount, proportions, Pedro's work, investing via a company?**

- Capital: at least **€3,000** (more for credibility/needs)
- Proportions: **free** (e.g. 50/50 – but beware **deadlock = paralysis**; add a deadlock clause)
- Pedro's **work** can't be capital (art. 58.2) → **ancillary commitment** (art. 86)
- **Yes**, they can invest through their own S.L. (legal persons can be members/founders)


**Q: Case 1: what is a **shareholders' agreement** (pacto de socios)?**

A **private contract** between (some) shareholders regulating their relationship beyond the by-laws. Binds **only signatories** (not the company/third parties), **confidential**, not registered.

> Not in the unit notes – needed for the case answer.


**Q: Case 1: typical content of a shareholders' agreement + when to sign?**

Governance (board seats, **reserved matters/veto**), **transfer restrictions** (lock-up, **right of first refusal**, **tag-along**, **drag-along**), **anti-dilution**, founders' **dedication & non-compete**, **vesting / good & bad leaver**, **deadlock** resolution, exit.
Sign **at incorporation** (founders) and **renegotiate** when investors enter.



## Case 2 – Patinetes Eléctricos S.L.

**Q: Case 2: equity €30,000, capital €90,000 (S.L.). Legal situation?**

Equity = **33% < ½ of capital** → **legal cause of mandatory dissolution** (art. 363.1.e LSC), unless capital is increased/reduced enough (or insolvency applies). (The **2/3 mandatory reduction** applies only to the **S.A.**)

> Calculation: 30,000 ÷ 90,000 = 0.33; threshold ½ × 90,000 = 45,000 > 30,000.


**Q: Case 2: are Pedro & María liable – as directors? as shareholders?**

**As directors**: yes, if they don't **convene the GM** (within **2 months**) to dissolve or fix the situation (or request judicial dissolution) → **liable for company debts arising after** the cause (subsidiary, joint & several).
**As shareholders**: **no** – capital company.


**Q: Case 2: other management systems? Would a board change their liability?**

S.L./S.A.: **sole director, joint & several, joint (S.A. max 2), board** (min 3; S.L. max 12). On a board the regime is the same: **all members who adopted** the harmful decision are **jointly and severally liable** unless they prove they opposed, didn't know, or did everything to avoid it (art. 237).


**Q: Case 2: how to reverse the capital impairment?**

- **Capital increase** (e.g. the Business Angel's €100,000 in cash; or offsetting shareholder loans)
- **Capital reduction to compensate losses** (S.L. capital ≥ €3,000)
- **Accordion** (reduction + simultaneous increase)
- Members' **contributions to equity** / returning to profit
Goal: equity **≥ ½ of capital**.


**Q: Case 2: type of capital increase for the Business Angel? Steps?**

A **cash** increase by **creating new stakes with a share premium**. Steps: **directors' report** & proposal → **GM resolution** (by-law amendment, S.L. > ½ of all votes) → **pre-emptive right** handled (waiver) → **subscription & payment** (bank certificate) → **public deed** → **registration** of resolution + execution.


**Q: Case 2: do Pedro & María have a pre-emptive right? How to manage it?**

**Yes** – cash increase. They **individually waive** it (or the GM **excludes** it) so the stakes can go to the Business Angel.


**Q: Case 2: BA invests €100,000 for 10%. Pre-money value?**

Post-money = 100,000 ÷ 10% = **€1,000,000** → pre-money = **€900,000** → **€10 per existing stake** (900,000 ÷ 90,000).


**Q: Case 2: capital €90,000 in €1 stakes. Fill in the capital increase for 10% at €100,000.**

New stakes N: N ÷ (90,000 + N) = 10% → **N = 10,000**.
- Increase: **€10,000**, 10,000 new stakes numbered **90,001–100,000**
- Total stakes **100,000** of €1
- Premium: **€90,000** total = **€9 per stake**
- Capital + premium = **€100,000**
- New capital **€100,000** (stakes 1–100,000)

> The **share premium** is the anti-dilution mechanism: the BA pays €10 per stake (€1 par + €9 premium) = the real value, so the founders' value isn't diluted. Bonus: equity becomes 30,000 + 100,000 = **€130,000 > ½ × 100,000** → the impairment is fixed.


**Q: Case 2: which company should absorb which?**

**PATINETES ELÉCTRICOS S.L. absorbs PTEL S.L.U.** – Patinetes holds the 40 employees, all client/supplier contracts, IP rights and **licences/permits**; absorbing the other way would require transferring all of these (and licences may not be transferable).


**Q: Case 2: what type of merger, and what can be avoided?**

**Merger by absorption of a wholly-owned subsidiary** (special merger, art. 53 RDLME): simplified draft; **no exchange ratio**, **no directors' and experts' reports**, **no capital increase** of the absorber, **no approval by the absorbed company's GM**.


**Q: Case 2: order of the merger procedure?**

1. **Common draft terms** signed by both managing bodies
2. **Publication/deposit** (Registry or websites) ≥ **1 month** before the GM
3. **Merger balance sheets** (≤ 6 months)
4. **GM approval** of Patinetes (within 6 months of the draft)
5. **Publication** of the resolution (BORME + newspaper, or individual notice)
6. **1-month creditor opposition** period
7. **Public deed** of merger
8. **Registration** → effective; PTEL extinguished & cancelled


**Q: Case 2: reasons for the merger?**

PTEL lost its purpose (only serves the parent): **simplify the structure**, **cut costs** (one set of accounts, filings, governance), **synergies**, concentrate employees/assets. Stating them (draft/report) helps **transparency** and justifies **valid economic reasons** (e.g. for tax neutrality).
