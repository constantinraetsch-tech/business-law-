## 9.1 Free competition

**Q: Why protect free competition?**

Market economy (art. **38 CE**) assumes competition allocates resources **most efficiently** → **lower prices, more quantity, variety and quality** for consumers. Pure freedom leads firms to **agree not to compete**, so the law must intervene.


**Q: Main Spanish competition law? Four controlled conducts?**

**Act 15/2007 on the Defence of Competition (LDC)**.
**Prohibited**: 1. **Collusive practices**, 2. **Abuse of dominant position**.
**Controlled**: 3. **Economic concentrations**, 4. **State aid**.



## 9.2 Collusive practices & abuse

**Q: What does art. 1 LDC prohibit?**

Any **agreement, collective decision or recommendation, or concerted or consciously parallel practice** that has the **object or effect** (actual or potential) of **preventing, restricting or distorting competition** in all or part of the **national market**.


**Q: Three conditions of a prohibited collusive practice?**

1. A **collusive practice** exists
2. **Actual or potential** restriction of competition
3. Effect on all/part of the **national market**


**Q: Expressly prohibited collusive practices (art. 1)?**

a) **Fixing prices** or trading conditions
b) **Limiting** production, distribution, development, investment
c) **Sharing markets** or sources of supply
d) **Discriminatory** conditions for equivalent transactions
e) **Tying** – supplementary obligations unrelated to the contract


**Q: Is intention relevant for a collusive practice?**

**No** – it's an **objective** prohibition: the mere **agreement capable** of restricting competition suffices; intent and actual effect are irrelevant. Also applies to foreign firms/acts **if effects reach the Spanish market**.


**Q: Consequence of a prohibited agreement? Exemption?**

**Null and void**. Exempted if it improves production/distribution or promotes **technical/economic progress**, with consumers getting a **fair share** of the benefits.


**Q: Is having a **dominant position** illegal?**

**No** – only its **abusive exploitation** (art. 2 LDC), even of a legal monopoly. Dominance = factual market power (market share), to be proven.


**Q: Examples of **abuse** of dominant position (art. 2)?**

a) Imposing **unfair prices** or conditions
b) **Limiting** production/development to the detriment of firms/consumers
c) Unjustified **refusal to supply**
d) **Unequal conditions** for equivalent services
e) **Tying** unrelated services
(Non-exhaustive list)


**Q: Conducts **exempted** from the prohibitions (arts. 4–6)?**

1. Conduct resulting from **application of a law**
2. Conduct of **minor importance** (de minimis)


**Q: **De minimis** thresholds (LDC Regulation, art. 1)?**

- **Competitors**: combined share **≤ 10%**
- **Non-competitors**: each **≤ 15%**
- Unclear: **10%** each
- **Cumulative** parallel networks: **5%** (only if networks cover ≥ **30%** of the market)


**Q: Which conducts are **never** of minor importance?**

**Price fixing, limiting production/sales, sharing markets/customers** between competitors, and **fixed or minimum resale prices** imposed on buyers (even between non-competitors).



## 9.3 Concentrations & State aid

**Q: What is an **economic concentration** (art. 7 LDC)? Is it prohibited?**

A **stable change of control** of all or part of one or more companies (mergers, acquisitions). **Not prohibited** – but **controlled** if it may restrict competition.


**Q: When must a concentration be notified (art. 8 LDC)?**

If **either**:
a) it acquires/increases a market share **≥ 30%**, or
b) combined Spanish turnover **> €240 million** and at least **two** participants each **> €60 million**


**Q: Concentration control procedure?**

1. **Notify the CNMC** before execution
2. **Suspension** – can't execute until authorised
3. CNMC **authorises**, authorises **with conditions**, or **prohibits**
Not notifying = **administrative offence**.


**Q: State aid under EU law?**

In principle **incompatible** with the internal market (privileged position). Automatically allowed: aid of a **social** nature or for **natural disasters**/exceptional events (art. 107.2 TFEU); possible for regional development, projects of common European interest, serious economic disturbance. **De minimis: ≤ €200,000 over 3 fiscal years.**


**Q: What does the Spanish LDC do about State aid?**

It does **not prohibit** it – the **CNMC** may **analyse** aid and its effects on competition and report. Breaches by a State can be taken to the **CJEU** by the Commission or another State.



## 9.4 Supervisory bodies & sanctions

**Q: Who supervises competition?**

**National** market → **CNMC** (Comisión Nacional de los Mercados y la Competencia); appeals to the **Audiencia Nacional**.
**Cross-border/EU** market → **European Commission** (DG Competition); appeals to the **General Court**, then the **Court of Justice**.


**Q: Sanctions under the LDC?**

Graduated: **minor** up to **1%**, … **very serious** up to **10%** of total **turnover** of the previous year. Plus up to **€60,000** for **legal representatives/managers** involved (art. 63).


**Q: What is the **leniency** programme?**

The **first** cartel member to provide decisive evidence gets **full immunity** from the fine; later ones can get a **reduction** (arts. 65–66 LDC).



## 9.5 Unfair competition

**Q: Which law regulates **unfair competition**? Purpose?**

**Act 3/1991 on Unfair Competition (LCD)**. Not against *intense* competition, but against competition by **dishonest means** harming competitors, the market or consumers.


**Q: Objective and subjective scope of the LCD?**

Any unfair act **before, during or after** a transaction, **whether or not the contract is concluded** (art. 2). Applies to entrepreneurs, professionals **and any person** acting in the market.


**Q: General clause of unfair competition (art. 4 LCD)?**

Any conduct **objectively contrary to good faith** (intention irrelevant). Presumed towards consumers if: (1) contrary to **professional diligence** and (2) it **distorts or may distort** the economic behaviour of the **average consumer**.


**Q: Is the list of unfair acts closed?**

**No** – listed acts are unfair per se; others are judged under the **general good-faith clause**.


**Q: Unfair acts addressed to a **specific competitor**?**

1. **Defamation** (denigration)
2. **Comparison**
3. **Imitation**
4. Exploiting another's **reputation**
5. Violation of **business secrets**
6. **Inducing breach** of contract


**Q: When is a statement about a competitor **defamation**?**

Public statements that may **harm its reputation**, unless **true, accurate and appropriate**. Statements on **personal matters** are unfair **even if true**.


**Q: When is **comparative advertising** lawful?**

(a) compares goods with the **same purpose**, (b) refers **objectively** to **essential, relevant** features, (c) is **true** and doesn't exploit the competitor's **reputation**.

> Case 3: the KFC vs McDonald's tweet.


**Q: When is **imitation** unfair?**

Imitation of non-exclusive creations is in principle free, but unfair if it creates **risk of association** by consumers or **unfairly exploits** the competitor's reputation or effort.


**Q: Violation of **business secrets** – which special law?**

Disclosing/using trade secrets under a duty of confidentiality or obtained unfairly. Special law: **Act 1/2019 on Trade Secrets** (implements Directive 2016/943).


**Q: When is **inducing breach of contract** unfair?**

Inducing workers, suppliers or customers to breach basic contractual duties. Inducing a *regular* termination is unfair only if aimed at **secrets**, uses **deception**, or aims to **eliminate a competitor**.


**Q: Unfair acts against the **proper functioning of the market**?**

1. **Misleading acts**
2. Acts of **confusion**
3. **Misleading omissions**
4. **Aggressive practices**
5. **Infringement of laws** (gaining a significant advantage)
6. **Sale below cost**


**Q: Misleading **act** vs act of **confusion**?**

**Misleading**: false info, or true info that can induce **error** about **essential characteristics**, influencing economic behaviour.
**Confusion**: creating confusion with a **competitor's** activity, products or establishment (incl. **risk of association**).


**Q: What are **misleading omissions**?**

Advertising inducing error by **omitting/hiding** necessary info, or giving it **unclear, unintelligible, ambiguous**, **at the wrong time**, or **subliminal** advertising.


**Q: When is **selling below cost** unfair?**

Only when done **systematically** and it **misleads** consumers about the price level of other products in the same store, or aims to **eliminate a competitor**.


**Q: Two groups of **unfair practices towards consumers** (arts. 19–31 LCD)?**

**Misleading** practices (arts. 20–27) and **aggressive** practices (arts. 28–31). Purpose: protect consumers' **freedom of choice**.


**Q: Examples of **misleading practices** towards consumers?**

1. Causing **confusion** with competitors' marks
2. False claims of **codes of conduct / quality marks**
3. **Bait advertising** ("Barcelona for €1" with almost no seats)
4. False claims about **properties** ("cures diseases")
5. **Pyramid schemes** (Ponzi/Madoff)
6. **Covert advertising** (advertorials not marked as ads)


**Q: Examples of **aggressive practices** towards consumers?**

1. **Coercion** – making consumers believe they can't leave without buying
2. **Harassment** – home visits ignoring requests to leave
3. **Unsolicited, repeated** offers by phone/e-mail (Margus newsletter example)
