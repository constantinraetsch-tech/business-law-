## 5.1 Directors' obligations & duties

**Q: What is the general competence of directors (art. 209 LSC)?**

To **manage and represent** the company. The law doesn't list specific acts – it sets **principles** for how they must act.


**Q: Within which framework must directors act?**

1. **Law** (legal provisions)
2. **General meeting & by-laws** (instructions not contrary to law)
3. **Duty of care** and **duty of loyalty** (arts. 225–232 LSC)


**Q: Examples of specific legal obligations of directors?**

**Prepare annual accounts**, **convene the ordinary GM** yearly, convene the **extraordinary GM** when mandatory (e.g. dissolution cause), request **insolvency** in time.


**Q: **Duty of care / diligence** (art. 225 LSC)?**

Act with the diligence of an **orderly entrepreneur** (ordenado empresario), considering the nature of the post; subordinate **personal interest** to the company's; **adequate dedication**; duty to **demand and right to obtain information**.


**Q: Five manifestations of the duty of care?**

1. **Prudence**
2. **Professional competence**
3. **Dedication**
4. **Information**
5. **Surveillance & control** (vigilance)

> Mnemonic **P-P-D-I-S**: "**P**edro **P**lans **D**aily **I**n **S**ilence".


**Q: Two perspectives of the "**orderly entrepreneur**" standard?**

1. **Prudence** – no risks beyond what's reasonable after planning/analysis
2. **Professional competence** – expertise appropriate to the company's complexity (a bank's directors need more than a small shop's)


**Q: **Duty of loyalty** (art. 227 LSC)?**

Perform duties with the loyalty of a **faithful representative**, in **good faith** and in the **best interest of the company**.


**Q: Basic obligations derived from loyalty (art. 228 LSC)?**

1. Use powers only for their **purpose**
2. **Secrecy** – even **after** leaving office
3. **Abstain** in conflicts of interest (except own appointment/removal)
4. **Personal responsibility**, independent judgement
5. **Avoid conflicts of interest** – **no competing** with the company


**Q: Can the by-laws limit the duty of loyalty? Can directors be exempted?**

The loyalty regime is **mandatory** – by-law clauses limiting it are void. But the **GM** may **exempt** a director in a specific case (use of assets, business opportunity, third-party advantage) when no damage to the company is expected (art. **230 LSC**).



## 5.2 Business judgment rule & risk monitoring

**Q: What is the **business judgment rule** (art. 226 LSC)?**

In **strategic and business decisions**, the orderly-entrepreneur standard is **deemed met** if the director acted:
1. in **good faith**
2. **without personal interest**
3. with **sufficient information**
4. following an **adequate decision procedure**

> It protects **managerial discretion**: a bad *result* alone does not mean a breach. It is a **rebuttable presumption** – whoever sues must prove the standards weren't met. Mnemonic **G-N-I-P**: Good faith, No interest, Information, Procedure.


**Q: Which decisions are **excluded** from the business judgment rule?**

Decisions that **personally affect other directors or related persons**, especially those authorising **art. 230** operations (conflicts of interest).


**Q: Why does the law protect managerial discretion?**

To **remove the fear** of liability for unsuccessful decisions, **encourage risk-taking**, and avoid members **interfering** in management via liability actions.


**Q: Why is **risk monitoring** part of the duty of care?**

Directors must have an internal **information system** that detects gradual deterioration (profitability, finance) **in time**. No monitoring → possible **liability in vigilando** (breach of duty of care). **Unforeseeable** events (e.g. **Covid-19**) are **not** a breach.


**Q: What is **D&O insurance** (mind map, Unit 5)?**

**Directors & Officers liability insurance**: the company buys a policy covering directors' personal liability and **legal defence costs**; it has **coverage limits and exclusions** (typically intentional/fraudulent acts).

> Not developed in the notes – just know what it is.



## 5.3 Liability of directors

**Q: Two types of directors' financial liability?**

1. **Liability for debts** (of the company, in crisis situations)
2. **Liability for damages** (any damage caused by breach)
(+ possible **criminal** liability, outside the course)


**Q: Is liability for company debts **direct** or **subsidiary**?**

**Subsidiary** – creditors can claim from directors only if the company **cannot** pay (not merely doesn't want to).


**Q: Two cases of **liability for debts**?**

1. Not filing for **insolvency** within **2 months** of insolvency → liable for debts arising **after** it should have been filed (culpable insolvency, art. 444 TRLC)
2. Not **convening the GM** to dissolve when losses reduce **equity < ½ of capital** (art. 363.1.e LSC) → liable for debts arising **after** that point

> Key: only **later debts**, not earlier ones.


**Q: Three requirements of liability for damages (art. 236 LSC)?**

1. Act/omission **contrary to law/by-laws or duties**, with **fraud or fault**
2. **Damage**
3. **Causal link**
Fault is **presumed** if the act is contrary to law or by-laws.


**Q: Director: "The GM approved my decision, so I'm not liable." Correct?**

**No.** Authorisation or ratification by the GM **never exonerates** (art. 236.2 LSC).


**Q: Who is a **de facto director**? Are they liable?**

A person who in reality performs director functions **without title**, with a **null/expired** title, or under another title, **or** the person whose **instructions** the directors follow. **Yes** – same liability as a de jure director (art. 236.3).


**Q: Board adopts a harmful resolution. Who is liable (art. 237 LSC)?**

**All members** who adopted/executed it, **jointly and severally** – except those who **prove** they (a) **expressly opposed** it, (b) were **unaware** of it, or (c) knowing it, did **everything possible** to avoid the damage.

> Burden of proof is on the **director**.



## 5.4 Liability actions

**Q: Two **liability actions** against directors?**

1. **Corporate action** (art. 238) – damage to the **company**
2. **Individual action** (art. 241) – **direct** damage to **members or third parties**


**Q: Who can bring the **corporate liability action**, in which order?**

1. The **company** (GM resolution – even **not on the agenda**; it implies **removal** of the directors)
2. **Members with ≥ 5%** if: GM not convened when requested, company doesn't sue within **1 month**, or GM voted against (for **loyalty** breaches they may sue **directly**)
3. **Creditors** if not brought by company/members and the company's assets are **insufficient** (art. 240)


**Q: Requirements of the **individual action**?**

- **Direct damage** to members/third parties
- **Act of directors** in office
- **Unlawfulness**
- **Causal link** – burden of proof on the claimant


**Q: Can **public bodies** (Tax Agency, Social Security) hold directors liable?**

Yes – besides the commercial regime, **tax** and **labour law** have their own rules making directors liable for unpaid taxes/contributions in certain cases.
